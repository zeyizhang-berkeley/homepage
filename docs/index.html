About Me

I’m a theoretical chemistry Ph.D. candidate at UC Berkeley and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, co-advised by Prof. Martin Head-Gordon and Dr. Jin Qian, and a Research Scientist Intern at ByteDance Seed–AI for Science.

I bridge quantum chemistry and AI to make molecular simulation more practical.

My AI for science research spans machine-learned Hamiltonians, densities and force fields, scientific benchmarks for LLMs, and agentic workflows linking simulations with spectroscopy.

My quantum chemistry research combines GPU computing, real-space DFT, quantum Monte Carlo, and multireference methods to study strong correlation, excited states, and spectroscopy.

For details, see Research, Software, and my CV.

Research

AI for Science

Machine Learning for Electronic Structure

Developing machine-learned Hamiltonians with SCF feedback, using learned densities to reduce SCF iterations and ML force-field preoptimization to accelerate DFT geometry optimization.

ML Hamiltonians · SCF self-distillation · ML densities · ML force fields

Scientific Benchmarks for LLMs

Evaluating molecular-structure inference with shuffled and counterfactual inputs, benchmarking LLM reasoning about reaction mechanisms, transition states, and synthesis, and contributing tasks to scientific-agent benchmarks.

Scientific reasoning · LLM evaluation

Agentic AI Workflows

Automating structure preparation, simulations, HPC execution, and multimodal spectroscopic analysis from natural-language instructions, connecting theory and experiment through a digital twin for gas-phase reactions.

Agentic AI · Scientific workflows · Digital twins · Multimodal spectroscopy

Quantum Chemistry

GPU-Accelerated Quantum Chemistry

Accelerating THC, AFQMC, and real-space DFT on GPUs for large-scale gas- (molecular) and condensed-phase (periodic) simulations.

GPU computing · THC · AFQMC · Real-space DFT · gas/condensed phase

Real-Space Kohn–Sham DFT

Combining GPU acceleration, ML-density SCF guesses, and MLFF geometry guesses for large-scale simulations of complex systems.

Real-space DFT · GPU computing · ML-assisted initialization · Large-scale simulation

Quantum Monte Carlo

Developing and using GPU-accelerated AFQMC to generate high-quality dataset for transition-metal compounds.

AFQMC · Transition-metal compounds

Multireference Methods and Strongly Correlated Systems

Studying both static and dynamic correlation with multireference methods like AFQMC and NOCI(-RSPT) for strong correlated systems like transition metal compounds.

Strong correlation · NOCI(-RSPT) · AFQMC · Transition-metal compounds

Excited-State Theory and Computational Spectroscopy

Developing ROKS-EDA and frozen MO guesses for charge-transfer excitations; studying low-lying excited states and photoionization of small molecules and cluster complexes.

ROKS-EDA · Charge transfer · Low-lying excited states · Photoionization

Publications

* Equal contribution

MolP2SBench: Do LLMs Really Reason from Molecular Properties to Structure?

Xie, Y., Shi, Z., Zhang, Z., Qi, Z., Dong, Y., Wang, Y., Xiao, X.

Submitted to ICLR 2027 (2026). [arXiv]

MolP2SBench evaluates how LLMs infer molecular structure from physical properties. By testing their reliance on the supplied evidence and their responses to changes in that evidence, it reveals strengths and limitations in property-grounded molecular reasoning.

On Policy Self Distillation for Learning Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian.

Li, Y.*, Zhang, Z.*, Bai, D., Gerstein, M.

Submitted to ICLR 2027 (2026). [OpenReview]

OPSD uses the self-consistent-field solver as a teacher for pretrained Hamiltonian models, turning corrections to their own predictions into learning signals. This iterative self-distillation improves accuracy and transferability while reducing dependence on costly reference calculations.

PARSEC.py: A Python-Based Real-Space Kohn–Sham Density Functional Theory Code Accelerated by Machine Learned Charge Density.

Zhang, Z., Perez, C. M., Kwon, P., Head-Gordon, M., Qian, J.

J. Comput. Chem. 47, e70482 (2026). [Paper]

PARSEC.py combines real-space Kohn–Sham DFT and machine-learned charge-density initialization in a modular Python framework. Molecular benchmarks show fewer self-consistent-field iterations with suitable density guesses, while GPU acceleration speeds up diagonalization and Hartree-potential calculations.

Ionization-Driven Oxygen–Oxygen Bond Formation in Mixed CO₂–H₂O Clusters: The Role of Solvation and Cluster Composition.

Dias, N.*, Zhang, Z.*, Lemmens, A. K., Wannenmacher, A., Qian, J., Head-Gordon, M., Ahmed, M.

J. Am. Chem. Soc. 148, 35681–35693 (2026). [Paper]

Photoionization experiments and density functional theory reveal how local hydration controls oxygen–oxygen bond formation in CO₂–H₂O clusters. Water-rich environments favor peroxide-forming reactions, while molecular rearrangement and evaporation provide an indirect route to hydrogen peroxide.

An Energy Decomposition Method for Electronic Excitations in Two-Determinant Restricted Open-Shell Kohn–Sham Theory.

Ling, H., Shen, H., Zhang, Z., Head-Gordon, M.

J. Chem. Theory Comput. 22, 385–398 (2026). [Paper]

An energy decomposition framework for two-determinant ROKS excited states separates primary excitation from orbital relaxation. By quantifying energy changes and charge redistribution through orbital analysis, it helps interpret valence, core, and charge-transfer excitations.

An Improved Guess for the Variational Calculation of Charge-transfer Excitations in Large Systems.

Bogo, N., Zhang, Z., Head-Gordon, M., Stein, C. J.

Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 27, 17533–17547 (2025). [Paper]

A freeze-and-release scheme combines constrained initial guesses with squared-gradient minimization for orbital-optimized DFT. Tests on a supramolecular coordination cage and dye–semiconductor complexes demonstrate convergence to targeted charge-transfer excited states while avoiding collapse to lower-energy states.

Real-space Kohn–Sham Density Functional Theory for Complex Energy Applications.

Zhang, Z., Liu, L., Glenna, D. M., Jana, A., Perez, C. M., Qian, J.

Chem. Commun. 61, 10273–10286 (2025). [Paper]

This review examines the foundations and computational advances of real-space Kohn–Sham DFT, with applications to interfacial spectroscopy and carbon capture. It discusses opportunities for large-scale electronic-structure simulations alongside current limitations and future directions for exascale computing.
