About

I build electronic structure methods and scientific software.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in quantum chemistry at UC Berkeley, advised by Prof. Martin Head-Gordon and Dr. Jin Qian, and a researcher at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.

My research spans GPU-accelerated real-space density functional theory, electronic structure methods for excited states, machine learning for quantum chemistry, and recursive self-improvement for science.

I develop PARSEC.py, contribute to Q-Chem, and am a Research Scientist Intern with ByteDance Seed–AI for Science, working on GPU algorithms, quantum-chemical reference data, and machine-learned density functionals.

Research & Software

Real-space electronic structure

PARSEC.py

Develop a Python-based real-space Kohn–Sham DFT code with ML density interfaces, multi-node/multi-GPU parallelization, and GPU-accelerated geometry optimization and molecular dynamics.

  • Integrate ML density models like ChargE3Net and SCDP, reducing SCF iterations by up to 26.7%.[3]
  • Converge DFT for a system with over 10,000 atoms (~40,000 valence electrons) in under 5 minutes on 16 A100 GPUs.[9]
  • Interface with ML force fields like UMA and MACE, reducing total relaxation time by up to 48%.[9]
  • Investigate X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy, interfacial chemistry, and CO₂ capture.[7]

GPU-accelerated quantum chemistry

Develop ByteQC and GPU4PySCF with tensor hypercontraction (THC) for HF, perturbation, and CC theory, reducing average runtime and GPU memory by 38% and 72% on A100 GPUs.

Electronic structure

State-Specific Excited-State Theories, Multireference Methods, and Strong Correlation

Develop electronic-structure methods for excited states and strongly correlated systems.

  • Develop energy decomposition analysis for excitations in two-determinant ROKS.[5]
  • Develop a frozen MO initial guess for orbital-optimized DFT of charge-transfer excitations.[6]
  • Study strongly correlated C₂ with HF, DFT, and multireference NOCI(-RSPT) versus MRCI+Q and experiment, achieving 32%/24% lower bond-length/energy MAEs than EOM-DIP-CCSD for low-lying states.[8]

Machine learning

Machine-Learned Hamiltonians (MLH)

Develop machine-learned models for electronic states and molecular properties.

  • Develop label-free post-training for flow-matching QHFlow2 models via short SCF corrections, improving orbital accuracy on QH9 and HELM and outperforming supervised fine-tuning in eight transfer settings.[2]
  • Develop an electronic force field by considering orbital response in non-self-consistent MLH.
  • Develop manifold-based flow-matching models for electronic states and molecular properties.
